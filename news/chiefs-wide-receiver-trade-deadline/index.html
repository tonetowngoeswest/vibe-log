Chiefs need for a WR persists as trade deadline approaches

Which targets make sense, and how much is the franchise willing to give?

From the archive. This story reflects reporting at its original publication date.

TH

By Tony Hernandez

Nov. 8, 20261 min read

Kansas City has reached the shopping-cart stage of its receiver problem. The need is obvious. The uncomfortable question is what the Chiefs should put on the checkout counter before trading closes at 4 p.m. Eastern on Nov. 10.

A Nov. 4 analysis published by NFL.com identified Indianapolis' Josh Downs and Jacksonville's Brian Thomas Jr. as possible fits. It projected a fourth-round pick for Downs and a third-rounder for Thomas. Those are analyst estimates, not asking prices announced by the clubs, and neither player should be treated as a completed deal wearing a temporary disguise.

In that assessment, the appeal is different for each. Downs offers separation and an underneath option, but his slot work could overlap with Rashee Rice and Travis Kelce. Thomas brings a larger outside target and more contractual runway, although persuading another AFC contender to strengthen Kansas City would be its own contact sport.

The same analysis described a group disrupted by Tyquan Thornton's ankle injury and Rice's hamstring absence, with Xavier Worthy still searching for consistency. Adding a name is easy on a fan's imaginary depth chart. Adding a receiver who can learn the offense quickly enough to matter is considerably less tidy.

Here is the judgment call: Kansas City should pay for a role, not a headline. An extra target helps only if he solves a problem the existing group cannot solve reliably. A bargain that duplicates three other players is still clutter, even when the receipt looks beautiful.

For now, the reasonable expectation is a search, not a coronation. The deadline offers a chance to improve the menu. It does not guarantee that the kitchen suddenly becomes a five-star operation.
