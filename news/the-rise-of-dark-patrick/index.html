The rise of 'Dark Patrick'

To succeed at this stage in his career, Patrick must enter dark mode.

TH

By Tony Hernandez

Chiefs News contributor

Join the conversation

Independent coverage.
Red and gold perspective.

Nov. 8, 20262 min read

Patrick Mahomes does not need a cape, a sinister laugh or a new identity. The Chiefs need something considerably less cinematic: a quarterback who can turn an ugly possession into a useful one. Call that version of him Dark Patrick — an editorial nickname, not a report about his mood.

The distinction matters after Kansas City's 28-24 loss at Denver on Nov. 1. The Chiefs' game center credited Mahomes with 321 passing yards and three touchdowns. Denver's recap described an unforgiving finish: Kansas City reached the 1-yard line, failed to score, committed a false start and surrendered a fourth-down sack. Impressive numbers did not buy a happy ending.

There is a blueprint for the less glamorous approach. In Kansas City's January 2024 playoff win at Buffalo, Mahomes completed 17 of 23 passes for 215 yards and two touchdowns. The Chiefs won 27-24. They did not need a passing-yardage fireworks show; they needed efficient possessions, timely scores and enough composure to survive the next swing.

Mahomes has already addressed the villain business. At his Super Bowl LIX media appearance in February 2025, he framed the Chiefs' reputation around winning rather than embracing a bad-guy costume. The point was straightforward: Success can make a team unpopular without changing how it approaches the game.

Here is the argument for dark mode now: Make the defense defend the boring stuff, too. Take the available completion. Avoid treating every stalled drive as an invitation to audition for a highlight reel. None of that guarantees a win, and it does not erase the responsibilities of the line, receivers or coaching staff.

The playful nickname works only if it describes discipline. The real plot twist would be making efficiency look menacing. Kansas City does not need its quarterback to become a different person. It needs the next crucial possession to end differently.
