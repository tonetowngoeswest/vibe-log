Analysis

Butker keeps the job as missed kicks raise the stakes

Andy Reid is sticking with his longtime kicker for Atlanta. With Justin Tucker on the practice squad, every attempt carries a little more scrutiny.

TH

By Tony Hernandez

Chiefs News contributor

Join the conversation

Independent coverage.
Red and gold perspective.

Nov. 12, 20261 min read

Harrison Butker will keep kicking for the Chiefs in Atlanta this week. That was Andy Reid’s decision Wednesday, according to KCTV5’s report from his news conference. The replacement question has an answer for Sunday, even if the larger conversation is still warming up on the sideline.

The numbers explain the noise. RotoWire’s update puts Butker at 12 makes in 19 field-goal attempts and 22 in 23 extra-point tries through eight games. For a team that may need every available point, seven missed field goals make an uncomfortable companion to the weekly game plan.

Distance deserves a place in the discussion. Yahoo Sports reported that all but one of those field-goal misses came from at least 50 yards. Those are difficult kicks. Still, the Chiefs have to decide which distances they can trust when choosing between a field-goal attempt, a punt and keeping the offense out.

The potential alternative is already in the building. Kansas City’s transaction log records Justin Tucker’s practice-squad signing on Oct. 28. His presence gives the club another option, but Reid’s announcement means Butker gets the next opportunity. Butker will try to turn that opportunity into steadier results.

Reid’s approach gives an established player room to recover. The football question is how much uncertainty the offense can absorb while that happens. The most useful evidence will come from the attempts themselves, particularly when possessions become scarce and field position makes the decision harder.

The assignment for Atlanta is settled: Butker keeps the job. What happens afterward will depend on the Chiefs’ decisions and his results. Butker has another chance to quiet a debate that follows him onto the field. Splitting the uprights would be an efficient opening statement.
